# Body and hand pose tracking

`@margoengine/rigging/pose-tracking` converts MediaPipe landmarks to normalized humanoid quaternion rotations. It has no camera permissions, inference model, MediaPipe runtime dependency or import-time side effects. The consuming app produces tracker results. The entry is optional and leaves core/stage untouched.

`solveBodyPose` consumes 33 landmarks; `solveHandPose` consumes 21 and explicit left/right handedness. Outputs include normalized local XYZW rotations, model-space orientations, per-bone confidence and the body hips centre when observable. Body output covers hips, spine/chest/upperChest/neck/head, shoulders, upper/lower arms, wrists, upper/lower legs and feet (20 bones). Hands each cover wrist plus all fifteen finger joints, including thumb metacarpal (16 bones). The combined pose has 50 distinct outputs because wrist outputs overlap. Hand frames take precedence over the body's coarse wrist estimate.

`readPoseTrackingFrame` adapts MediaPipe Tasks: first body, and highest-confidence labelled left and right hands, preferring world landmarks. It carries handedness score into landmark confidence. Legacy Holistic fields and explicit world-hand fields are accepted by `PoseTracker.push`. In multi-person scenes, callers must associate hands with the intended body before passing results.

Coordinate defaults: MediaPipe x right, y down, z away is rotated to Margo x right, y up, z toward the camera. `coordinateSpace: 'margo'` uses input directly. `aspect` (default 1) scales normalized image x and z to image-height units; supply video width/height. Tasks/world body and hand frames ignore image aspect. `mirror` (default false) reflects x and swaps sides. All confidence is in [0,1]; absent confidence defaults to 1, and `minConfidence` defaults to 0.5. `upperBodyOnly` defaults false and suppresses legs/feet when enabled.

Two observed directions form an orthonormal frame, then a unit quaternion. Reference directions are normalized before the 1e-8 angular-collinearity check, keeping hand frames independent of input scale. Arm/leg zero-direction guards use a 1e-8 input-unit threshold. Limb directions determine swing; the bend plane supplies arm twist, shortest-arc transport of the torso supplies the straight-limb reference, and the palm plane supplies wrist/finger twist. A collapsed primary segment or palm frame has no rotation output; vertical arms, horizontal side kicks and right-angle finger curls retain their observable swing through transported reference frames. Local rotations divide out the nearest observed parent's model-space orientation. No anatomical angle clamp silently changes the observation. Depth ambiguity and roll around an isolated straight segment cannot be recovered from landmarks alone; the documented torso/palm references resolve that degree of freedom. Occluded/low-confidence measurements are omitted rather than converted to fabricated identity rotations.

`PoseTracker` holds the latest frame and advances independently of camera rate. Defaults: 0.05 second exponential quaternion smoothing; lost input holds for 0.2 seconds, then fades to neutral. A zero delta never advances a fade; zero smoothing follows immediately. `calibrate` captures the latest goal as neutral, including hips origin, so arbitrary camera and open-hand orientations map to the avatar's authored rest. `reset` clears filtered state, keeping goal/calibration; `clearCalibration` restores canonical interpretation. Results own their arrays; invalid frames reject before replacing the previous goal.

`addPoseTracking(animation, avatar, options)` lazily binds to the existing optional animation Retarget. Bind while the avatar is in its desired neutral/T-pose, push a neutral capture and calibrate, then feed captures. It retains authored target lengths and handles rest axes/missing optional bones. `hipScale` defaults to target/source hips height; `autoUpdate` defaults true. With `vrm`, a pose source precedes gaze/expressions/springs; otherwise the model's own scene advances it. `autoUpdate: false` enables manual stepping. AbortSignal checks happen before and after the optional module load, before attachment. Disposal restores only transforms it wrote, removes its source/plugin, and is idempotent; model disposal detaches without writing destroyed nodes. Give one producer ownership of a bone.

Hips translation is relative tracker-space data, never inferred absolute camera depth. MediaPipe's root-centred world landmarks therefore keep the avatar in place unless the caller supplies root motion or uses an external camera/world calibration. This avoids presenting a monocular projection heuristic as measured world position. A calibrated pose is needed for exact open-hand neutral alignment on different rigs. Face expressions/gaze remain the adjacent face/VRM entries' job.

## Evidence and comparison

The opaque Kalidokit 1.1.5 public UMD was executed in the separate engine-bench directory, not saved or inspected. Three body cases and six hand cases are retained in `docs/whitepaper/results/batch213-pose-public-values.json`. Both solvers expose wrist plus 15 finger joints per hand. Kalidokit has 12 top-level body outputs; Margo has 20 explicit bone rotations including head/feet and optional torso/shoulder nodes. The tested index bends are 0, 45 and about 63.435 degrees in both after coordinate sign conversion; the regression checks magnitudes to 1e-12. Margo returns normalized parent-relative quaternions rather than Kalidokit's Euler rigging conventions and does not reproduce its global-position heuristic or anatomical clamps.

Independent tests reconstruct observed limb/finger directions and prove rigid-camera rotation covariance for 20 orientations, normalized frames, mirroring, image/world aspect handling, confidence/degenerate input, frame-rate composition, calibration, loss, invalid-input atomicity, cancellation and native lifecycle. Real, licensed Seed-san and VRM1_Constraint_Twist_Sample render all 50 solved bone outputs with MToon and GPU skinning on native WebGPU and the full WebGL2 compatibility layer. Neutral calibration and disposal both restore exactly. No speed benchmark or timing claim is made; this small per-avatar solver reuses TypeScript quaternion and retarget primitives rather than adding a geometry/codec hot loop.

Entry size: 0 B before, 4,872 B gzip after; ceiling 5,128 B. Additional splits would fragment a single small solver/binding contract. Core plus stage remains 24,209 B. Camera/inference model downloads are owned by the consuming app.

## Clean-room sources

- Ivan Grishchenko, Valentin Bazarevsky, Andrei Zanfir, Eduard Gabriel Bazavan, Mihai Zanfir, Richard Yee, Karthik Raveendran, Matsvei Zhdanovich, Matthias Grundmann and Cristian Sminchisescu, *BlazePose GHUM Holistic: Real-time 3D Human Landmarks and Pose Estimation* (2022), https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11678v1. Landmark topology, hips-centred metric camera frame, ambiguity and distinction between landmark inference and model-specific pose lifting.
- Fan Zhang, Valentin Bazarevsky, Andrey Vakunov, Andrei Tkachenka, George Sung, Chuo-Ling Chang and Matthias Grundmann, *MediaPipe Hands: On-device Real-time Hand Tracking* (2020), https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10214v1. 21-point topology, handedness/presence and joint-angle geometry. No neural model or companion implementation was copied.
- Google AI Edge, *Pose Landmarker* and *Hand Landmarker* (living documentation), https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker and https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker. Tasks result shapes, normalized vs metric world landmarks and confidence.
- Gery Casiez, Nicolas Roussel and Daniel Vogel, *1 Euro Filter: A Simple Speed-based Low-pass Filter for Noisy Input in Interactive Systems*, CHI (2012), https://cristal.univ-lille.fr/~casiez/publications/hdrcasiez.pdf. Compared adaptive cutoff/lag tradeoffs with fixed-time exponential smoothing; the latter is used here for a deterministic, frame-rate-composable default. No companion code read.
- Ken Shoemake, *Animating Rotation with Quaternion Curves*, SIGGRAPH (1985), https://doi.org/10.1145/325334.325242. Unit quaternion interpolation, reused through Margo's existing quaternion API.
- VRM Consortium, *About pose data compatibility* (2024), https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm_animation-1.0/how_to_transform_human_pose.md. Normalized pose to target rest axes and missing optional-bone handling, via Margo's existing Retarget.
- Yeemachine, *Kalidokit public API documentation* (1.1.5), https://www.npmjs.com/package/kalidokit?activeTab=readme. Body/hand feature comparison and opaque numeric reference execution only.
