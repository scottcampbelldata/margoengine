# Physics audit across the engine

Margo's physics is held to one bar: the best open-source engine for documentary film work. A shot must re-render bit
for bit, nothing may jitter or pop at rest, and ships, water, wind, weather, terrain, destruction and characters must
behave by physical law on camera. The rigid and soft solvers are tracked row by row in
[rapier-parity.md](rapier-parity.md). This file tracks everything else in the engine that should obey physics and does
not yet.

**How this list was made (2026-11-25).** Physics was on hold while the engine used Rapier, so physical behaviour outside
the physics packages was never checked. Six read-only reviews then traced every package that moves something or
simulates a physical process, down to the code:

- water and weather;
- terrain, erosion and voxels;
- effects, explosions and audio;
- characters, animals and crowds;
- the film pipeline, determinism, import and export;
- wind, rain and water coupling across packages.

Every entry names the code it rests on (file and line), the physical root cause, what a correct version needs, and its
sources. The central claims were re-read in the code before this file was written: the dropped simulation time in
`physics.ts` and the force-free `Floater` in `ships.ts`.

**Status key.** OPEN: not fixed. BUILDING: work in progress. FIXED: landed, with the commit and the regression test.
Priority: P1 breaks film correctness (re-renders, stability or the ship-and-water core), P2 is a visible physical
shortcoming on camera, P3 is depth and completeness.

**The pattern.** The same root cause repeats across the engine: a kinematic or scripted stand-in where force-based
dynamics are needed. Examples are a ship that follows the water with no forces, wind sampled once at the camera, debris
on precomputed paths, craters stamped from a hand-set radius, and characters that never touch the physics world. Each
looks plausible in a still frame, so no test caught it. The fix for the class is in the last section: coupling tests
in the merge gate.

## The solver plan

The framework every solver is built on (clock, shared memory, field registry, solver interface, coupling scheduler,
shared kernels, determinism, snapshot and bake, threads) is specified in [physics-framework.md](physics-framework.md). The full
catalog of 103 solvers, with methods, kernels and the coupling matrix, is in [physics-solvers.md](physics-solvers.md). Problems seen in the demo videos and films are tracked shot by shot in [physics-demo-review.md](physics-demo-review.md).

Fixing the findings one by one would leave the engine as it is now: systems that look right alone and never affect
each other. So the physics is rebuilt as a set of solvers that share one clock, one set of physical fields and explicit
coupling contracts. Each solver is research grade, deterministic on the CPU (Rust compiled to WebAssembly, bit-identical
re-renders), steps on the film clock, supports snapshot, restore and bake, and has a "film" accuracy setting for
offline renders. A GPU preview may follow where it helps, but the CPU path is the reference. Speed is not tuned until
the features are complete.

### Shared foundation (lands first, everything depends on it)

| Quantity | Owner | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Film clock | the water and particles work (PA-07) | One simulated time `t` and integer step index for every solver; `advance(frame, fps)` never drops time (PA-01, PA-02). |
| Gravity | the dynamics work (PA-08) | One gravity value in `@margoengine/math`, passed to every solver and shader as a uniform. |
| Wind | the atmosphere solver | `WindField.sample(x, y, z, t)` returns the local air velocity (and density and temperature). Every consumer samples at its own position: bodies, cloth, sails, trees, particles, spray, smoke, the sea's wave growth. |
| Water surface | the water solver | A pure, synchronous `seaSurface(x, z, t)` returning height, normal, orbital and current velocity, plus river and flood depth and velocity (PA-11). Bodies write back pressure and volume sources (hull displacement, impacts). |
| Terrain | the terrain and destruction work | One bed height that erosion, slides and craters write and colliders read (PA-37). |
| Rain | the atmosphere solver | Rain rate in mm/h with a drop-size distribution, per position (PA-23). |

## The guard against the class

Each fix lands with a regression test. Coupling tests then become a required check in `check:merge`. For every system
that claims a physical effect, they assert that:

- force goes in and motion comes out, both ways where the physics is two-way (a ship heaves more in a heavier sea, a
  sail pulls harder in more wind, a body in a flood is pushed and pushes back);
- energy and momentum behave (no gain against a static boundary, momentum conserved in collisions);
- results are independent of the frame rate (the same shot at 24, 30 and 60 fps gives identical physics);
- a resumed render is bit-identical to an uninterrupted one.
