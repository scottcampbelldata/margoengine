WebGPU and WebGL2, side by side

The same scene, the same engine code: the left view draws with your browser's WebGPU, the right one with the WebGL2 compatibility layer (@margoengine/webgl2-compat), which a browser without WebGPU downloads instead. The numbers below are measured in your browser, on your GPU, while you watch.

d is the largest red, green or blue difference of a pixel, 0 to 255, between the two views after both have drawn the same frame. Rendering through two shader compilers can move a value by a level or two, so the engine counts a pixel as different only above 2; with MSAA on, the two paths resolve edges with their own sample patterns, which shows along silhouettes. Frame times are medians of the last 60 frames: the CPU time of the engine's render call, and the time until the GPU reports the frame's work done. The layer runs every WebGPU call through WebGL2, so it costs more CPU time. How each site demo compares is in the compatibility page.
