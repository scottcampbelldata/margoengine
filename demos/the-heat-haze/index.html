The Heat Haze

Hot air on the asphalt turns grazing rays back up, so the far road shows the sky.

Watch the far road. As the asphalt air warms, a pool of mirrored sky and posts opens where the road meets the hills, and it grows with the heat. This is a long-lens view, the way road mirages are photographed.

About the effect

The heat sits in a layer a few centimetres deep on the asphalt, as Hagen and colleagues measured in 2026. Margo traces every ray through it. A ray that leaves the eye flatter than the mirage edge (0.37° at +28 °C, 0.19° at +7 °C) turns back up and shows the sky. Convection eddies drifting in the wind make the edge flicker.
