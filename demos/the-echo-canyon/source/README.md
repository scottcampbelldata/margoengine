# The Echo Canyon

Move the receiving station across a canyon and send a test pulse. The white path is direct. The copper and blue paths reflect once from the two rock faces. The readout measures each reflected path's added travel time.

The model uses the image-source construction. Reflect the source across the wall plane, draw a straight line from the listener to that image, and recover the turn point where the line crosses the wall. The direct and image lengths determine the travel times at 343.2 m/s. The listening option plays the three signals at their relative arrival times. It scales their amplitude by distance and fixed illustrative wall reflection factors. The visual pulses travel at one-quarter speed.

The canyon is an original landscape inspired by layered Colorado Plateau sandstone. The numerical model uses two local planar faces and omits higher-order arrivals, atmospheric refraction, ground scattering and diffraction.
