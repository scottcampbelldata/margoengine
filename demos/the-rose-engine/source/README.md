# The Rose Engine

Select a six-lobe or nine-lobe rosette, then turn the spindle or run the engraving pass. The rosette and workpiece share the same rotating spindle. The fixed point follower holds the outer rim of the rosette at one position. The headstock therefore traverses sideways, and the tool traces the gold line on the turning workpiece. The existing inner grooves represent earlier passes made at different radial tool settings.

The exhibit uses a translating headstock to isolate the radial motion. Historical rose engines often rocked the headstock against a follower, sometimes called the rubber. For a rosette with n lobes, let m(φ) = A cos(nφ) + B cos(2nφ). The cam radius is 198 mm + m(φ). At spindle angle θ the follower reads m(−θ), so the headstock shifts by −m(−θ). The fixed tool 125 mm from the nominal center makes a cut at radius 125 mm + m(−θ) and workpiece angle −θ. In world space the live groove ends at the cutter tip.

The fundamental lobe repeat is 360°/n because the harmonic indices are n and 2n, with greatest common divisor n and a nonzero fundamental. That gives 60° or 40°. The radial profile repeats many times during one turn, and the spatial curve closes after 360°. The groove is a geometric rendering of the ideal cut. The exhibit omits friction, tool wear, chips and cutting load.

## Review the exhibit

1. At the normal view, move the spindle slider and watch the brass rosette stay against its follower as the headstock slides.
2. Turn on Workpiece close-up. Move the slider from 0° to 360° and watch the gold cut end at the fixed tip and close at one turn.
3. Switch from 6 lobes to 9 lobes. Compare the 60° and 40° repeat readings and the corresponding workpiece patterns.
