# The Seismograph

Set a tremor frequency between 0.35 and 3.20 Hz. The shaking table and chart recorder share the base motion. A spring-mounted inertial mass carries the stylus. Its position relative to the base becomes the pen trace, with the newest sample under the tip. A moving chart window shows the past 3.6 seconds. Pause recording to inspect the instrument. With reduced motion enabled, the frequency control instead previews the selected steady response until recording is started.

The ground follows a synthetic sine wave with a fixed 21 mm amplitude. The relative displacement r solves r″ + 2ζω₀r′ + ω₀²r = −g″, where g is the ground displacement, the natural frequency is 1.20 Hz and the damping ratio is 0.21. For each frequency the solution combines the sinusoidal steady response and an analytically decaying transient. Changing frequency preserves the base displacement, absolute mass position and absolute mass velocity. The settled pen amplitude readout is the magnitude of the steady response, in millimetres.

This is a one-axis instructional instrument with original geometry. It models ideal spring and damping behavior. The motion is deliberately large enough to see. The input has no earthquake magnitude or real event data.
