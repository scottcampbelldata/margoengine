# The Section Saw

The model is an original static shaft cartridge, assembled from a steel spindle, anodized housing, bronze bushings, ceramic sleeve, elastomer seals and fasteners. All dimensions use meters inside Margo and millimeters on the control. The demonstration focuses on how a mechanical cross-section is read.

Choose **Along the shaft** or **Across the shaft**, and slide the cutting plane through the cartridge. The hatched surfaces are generated by Margo’s depth-fail winding pass on the actual closed component meshes. The parts count reports how many of the 22 modeled solid pieces the current plane intersects. **Hidden edges** adds dashed feature segments only where they are occluded by the remaining geometry.

A live plane normalized by Margo Materials is shared by the assembly section pass and both line passes. The same turned meshes, open inlay strips, crease segments and camera views run on desktop and phone. The open inlays clip with the assembly and leave the stencil winding for the solid pieces. This is a section drawing model, without pressure, sealing forces or load ratings.

## Browser proof

Desktop WebGPU and cold 390 px phone sessions on native WebGPU, forced WebGL2 and automatically selected WebGL2 all passed a real slider input, both cutting directions, the hidden-edge toggle, travel to the fully intact end and back. The reference poses return ten solids on the center longitudinal plane, four at the center crosswise plane and zero at +166 mm. Browser screenshots also verify that the hatched surfaces are present in both section views and disappear at the end.

With the gallery module preloads and 150 ms latency, 1.6 Mbps download and fourfold CPU slowdown, the first useful scrub completed at 1.55 s on native WebGPU, 2.75 s on forced WebGL2 from a fresh browser and 2.35 s on automatically selected WebGL2 in the next fresh network context. Desktop WebGPU completed at 0.62 s.
