# The Joint Workshop

Drive both doors or move either one independently. The motors ask for 95.1° of rotation, and the joint stops hold each door at 64.2°. Tap the bob to apply an impulse to the five-link chain. Show joint frames to see the axes and limit arcs from Margo PhysicsDebugView. Reset restores the closed doors and the stationary chain.

The cabinet is about 4.65 m wide. Two conventional revolute joints move rigid door leaves. The pendulum uses five conventional ball joints with independent rotation-vector limits of ±0.27, ±0.17, and ±0.27 rad. Gravity acts on the linked bodies. One Margo Rust/Wasm solver advances both rigs at 120 fixed steps per simulation second. Rendered transforms are read from the rigid bodies. No multibody mode is required. The thin offset on the debug markers keeps the joint lines legible over the cabinet hardware. [Margo Engine public main, verified revision](https://github.com/scottcampbelldata/margoengine/tree/0efd9cff8230507a32deb49907ecd499d46925f5). [David Baraff, Linear-Time Dynamics using Lagrange Multipliers, CMU, 1996](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/linear-time-dynamics-using-lagrange-multipliers).

On both WebGPU and direct Margo WebGL2, the same geometry, poses, PBR finishes and joint-line data are rendered. The solver selects its actual supported WASM variant. The public exhibit preloads that variant along with the small scene module. The browser verification operates both doors, verifies a physical stop, independently closes the left leaf, impulses the pendulum, renders and clears the debug view, then resets the system.
